Executive SummaryBOCC (Business Orbit Campus Clubs) is a web platform that helps college students turn a startup idea into a real, working business over roughly 12 months.Instead of students figuring things out on their own, BOCC gives them a clear, step-by-step path with 5 stages. At every stage, real people (the Business Orbit team, mentors, incubators, and investors) check the student's work, give feedback, and only let them move forward once they're ready.Along the way, students can also:Talk to mentors for advicePitch to investors for fundingApply to incubators for support and spaceTake up freelance work posted by companies, so they can earn money while building their startupThe platform has 6 types of users: Student, Mentor, Investor, Incubator, Company, and Admin (Business Orbit staff). Each one gets their own dashboard and set of permissions.2. Problem StatementToday, a college student who wants to build a startup usually struggles with:No clear roadmap — they don't know what to do first, second, or third.No accountability — nobody checks their progress, so many give up halfway.No access to mentors or investors — these people are hard to reach without the right network.No easy way to build a real product — most students don't know how to find developers or manufacturers.No money to support themselves while spending time on an unproven idea.Scattered tools — students end up using WhatsApp, Google Forms, spreadsheets, and email all separately, with nothing connected.BOCC solves this by putting the entire journey — guidance, review, mentorship, funding, incubation, and income — into one connected platform.3. Product Vision“Every college should have its own startup factory.”BOCC wants to become the standard platform that colleges use to turn student ideas into real companies — with built-in checks at every step, so quality stays high and students actually finish what they start.Each college gets its own BOCC Chapter, so the platform can scale to hundreds of colleges while keeping each chapter's community close-knit.4. Business GoalsGet as many college chapters onboarded as possible (growth goal).Increase the number of students who complete all 5 stages and launch a real startup.Build a strong network effect: more mentors and investors attract more students, and more students attract more mentors/investors/companies.Create revenue streams, for example:Chapter licensing fees paid by colleges/incubatorsA small commission on freelance projects completed through the Companies modulePartnership fees from incubators/investors who want visibility on the platformBuild a reusable, provable success story (“X startups launched”, “$Y raised by students”) that helps sell BOCC to more colleges.5. Non-Functional RequirementsScalability: The system must support many colleges (chapters) at once, each potentially with thousands of students, without one chapter's data leaking into another's.Performance: Key pages (dashboard, mentor/investor listings) should load in under 2 seconds under normal load.Availability: Target 99.5%+ uptime, since students rely on deadlines and reviews.Accessibility: Follow WCAG 2.1 AA guidelines so the platform is usable by people with disabilities.Device support: Fully responsive — usable on mobile, tablet, and desktop, since many students will use phones.Data retention: Stage submissions, chat logs, and approvals must be kept for the life of the platform (for accountability/audit).Localization-ready: Text should be stored in a way that supports adding other languages later, even if only English ships first.Auditability: Every approval/rejection/verification action must be logged with who did it and when.10. Acceptance Criteria (examples)Stage SubmissionGiven a student is on an unlocked stage, when they upload all required items and click Submit, then the stage status changes to “Under Review” and Admin is notified.Given a stage is “Under Review,” when the student tries to submit again, then the system blocks a duplicate submission until a decision is made.Stage ApprovalGiven Admin approves a stage, when the approval is saved, then the next stage becomes unlocked for that student and the student receives a notification.Given Admin rejects a stage, when the rejection is saved with comments, then the student can view the comments and resubmit.Mentor Chat TimeoutGiven a chat request has been open for 14 days with no mentor reply, when the deadline passes, then the student sees an “Escalate to Support” option.Company Opportunity ApprovalGiven a company submits an opportunity missing a required field, when they try to submit, then the system blocks submission and highlights the missing field.Given Admin approves an opportunity, when the approval is saved, then the opportunity appears in the public Freelance listing.Access ControlGiven a student views a mentor or investor profile, when the page loads, then no direct contact information (phone/email) is shown anywhere.17. Security ConsiderationsHidden contact info: Mentor/investor phone numbers and emails must never be exposed to students through the UI or API responses — enforce this at the backend, not just the frontend.Role-based access control (RBAC): Every API endpoint must check the user's role before returning data.Verified-only access: Unverified accounts should have no access beyond a “pending” holding page.File upload safety: Scan all uploaded files (idea documents, survey data, pitch decks, product photos) for malware before storage.Data encryption: Encrypt sensitive data at rest and use HTTPS everywhere in transit.Student data protection: Since users are college students, treat personal data with extra care (similar in spirit to data-privacy regulations like GDPR/FERPA) — minimize what's collected, and clearly explain how it's used.Audit trail: Every approval, rejection, and verification action must be logged with actor, timestamp, and reason.Session &amp; password security: Use strong password rules, secure session tokens, and support account recovery safely.Rate limiting: Prevent spam on chat requests, opportunity applications, and pitch decks.23. Risks &amp; AssumptionsRisksThe 14-day mentor response process is manual and relies on Business Orbit staff following up — this may not scale well as the number of chapters grows.Manual stage review by Business Orbit could become a bottleneck if the number of students grows faster than the review team.There may not be enough mentors/investors relative to the number of students (supply-demand imbalance).Data privacy rules can differ by region/country if BOCC expands beyond one country.Quality control across many different college chapters could vary.AssumptionsBusiness Orbit will grow its internal review/ops team in step with student growth, at least until more of the review process is automated.Colleges are willing to partner and set up official BOCC chapters.Students have reasonably reliable internet access to use the platform.All approvals are manual for the initial version (v1); automation can be added later (see Future Scope).25. Technical RecommendationsFrontend: A modern component-based framework (e.g., React/Next.js) for a fast, responsive experience across mobile and desktop.Backend: A standard REST API backend (e.g., Node.js or Python/Django), organized so each role's permissions are enforced server-side.Database: A relational database (e.g., PostgreSQL) — the data here is highly structured (users, stages, approvals) and benefits from strong relationships and constraints.File storage: Cloud object storage (e.g., AWS S3 or equivalent) for uploads like pitch decks, product photos, and documents.Chat: Either build a simple in-house messaging system or use a reliable third-party chat API, since real-time delivery and history matter.Notifications: An email service provider (e.g., SendGrid or similar) plus in-app notifications; a background job scheduler to handle timed events like the 14-day mentor SLA.Multi-tenancy: Design the database and permissions so each Chapter's data is logically separated, even though it lives in one shared system.Hosting: Any major cloud provider (AWS, GCP, or Azure) with autoscaling, since usage will likely spike around admission cycles and stage deadlines.Rollout approach: Launch with one or two pilot chapters first, validate the 5-stage flow and admin workload in practice, then expand to more colleges.End of PRD.
